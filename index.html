Australia's Digital Divide

Government services are increasingly digital, but not every Australian has the same ability to access, afford and confidently use the technologies needed to benefit from that shift.

01 · The digital shift

Australia is becoming a digital-government society

Most interactions with Services Australia now happen digitally. But the level of digital self-service still differs substantially between programs.

Digital interactions have stabilised at around 95% under the current measurement method. The methodology changed in 2024–25, so the earlier result is shown only as context.

Source: Services Australia, Annual Report 2025–26.

Health services are almost entirely self-managed digitally, while Child Support remains considerably less digital.

Source: Services Australia, Annual Report 2025–26.

02 · Experience matters

Digital does not always mean a better experience

Digital channels dominate service delivery, yet customers still report their highest satisfaction when receiving face-to-face assistance.

Face-to-face service scores 88.0, compared with only 73.3 for online service. The agency average provides a useful benchmark between them.

Source: Services Australia, Annual Report 2025–26.

Satisfaction improved across every channel in 2025–26, but the ranking of the channels did not change.

Sources: Services Australia, Annual Report 2024–25 and Annual Report 2025–26.

Fair treatment performs strongly across programs, while time and effort remain major weaknesses, particularly for Child Support.

Source: Services Australia, Annual Report 2025–26.

03 · Place shapes participation

But can every Australian participate equally?

Australia may be highly digital overall, but digital inclusion is not evenly distributed across the country.

What is the ADII? The Australian Digital Inclusion Index measures whether people can access digital technologies, afford appropriate connectivity, and use digital tools confidently. Scores run from 0 to 100; higher scores indicate greater digital inclusion.

The ACT records the highest 2025 Digital Inclusion Index score at 79.5, while Tasmania records the lowest at 69.4.

Sources: 2025 Australian Digital Inclusion Index. Map geometry: Australia GeoJSON.

The First Nations digital inclusion gap is largest in the Northern Territory (17.3 points) and Western Australia (15.1 points), where remote and very remote communities make up a larger share of the population.

Sources: 2025 First Nations Digital Inclusion Snapshot. Map geometry: Australia GeoJSON.

A population-scaled view shifts attention away from Australia's largest land areas. New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland dominate the population picture, while the Northern Territory becomes comparatively small.

Sources: ABS, National, state and territory population (March 2026); 2025 ADII.

04 · Distance compounds the gap

Distance still shapes digital opportunity

State averages hide an important geographic pattern: digital inclusion generally declines as communities become more remote, with the largest disadvantage appearing in very remote Australia.

Very remote Australia records an overall ADII score of 62.5. Access is the sharpest weakness, falling to 57.4 compared with 78.3 in major cities.

Source: 2025 Australian Digital Inclusion Index.

Capital cities outperform the rest of Australia across every 2025 ADII dimension. Using the report's unrounded estimates, the largest difference is in Digital Ability at 7.8 points.

Source: 2025 Australian Digital Inclusion Index.

Between 2023 and 2025, Australia's Access score increased by 4.8 points, while Digital Ability increased by 8.7 points. The country moved simultaneously toward better connectivity and stronger digital skills.

Sources: 2025 ADII and 2023 ADII.

05 · Who is left behind?

Who risks being left behind?

Australia's digital transition is progressing, but exclusion remains concentrated among groups that already face social and economic barriers.

Two-thirds of Australians aged 75 and over are digitally excluded or highly excluded. Exclusion is also more than double the national rate among people who did not complete secondary school and public-housing residents.

Source: 2025 Australian Digital Inclusion Index.
